# Picture-Frame Platformer Decision Ledger

## Batch 068

Questions 336-340 received no user reply. These selections define the
provisional shared visual grammar for safe platforms, hazards, interactables,
portals, and semantic lighting. They remain open to direct user override and
grant no asset generation, runtime implementation, rights-sensitive reuse,
push, or deployment authority.

### 336. Shared Platform Visual Grammar

Selection: every safe landing uses one continuous high-contrast top lip and
double ink-or-material edge over its route material. Static platforms keep an
unbroken quiet edge, moving platforms add paired directional notches and a
visible path cue, and temporary or revealable platforms use a torn or dashed
secondary edge plus stable state cue. Matching non-color descriptions preserve
meaning while semantic collision remains independent from art meshes and LODs.

Rationale:

- Current routes still use generic shape, color, and glow bindings rather than
  a finished platform family.
- One stable edge language preserves instant recognition across eight distinct
  material recipes without flattening them into identical blocks.
- Renderer geometry cannot safely own collision, reachability, or route state.

### 337. Shared Hazard Visual Grammar

Selection: hazards never use the safe double top lip. Their bounds use one
broken edge, inward triangular cuts, and crosshatch over the local route
material, with fixed dormant, warning, and active meanings. Moving hazards add
a path cue and shadow hazards retain their rune identity. Accessible labels and
optional audio or haptic intents mirror the same semantic phases while hazard
volumes remain independent from meshes, effects, and LODs.

Rationale:

- Current hazards are generic interactive targets whose route color and glow
  do not prove a safe-versus-harmful distinction.
- Shape, texture, and deterministic telegraph phases survive color and motion
  differences.
- A separate semantic volume keeps collision and timing replayable.

### 338. Shared Interactable Visual Grammar

Selection: every interactable uses stable four-corner brackets, dotted touch
texture, a route-specific semantic symbol, and concise label. Open brackets
mean available, an inner bracket and thicker edge mean focused, a matching
symbol and path mean valid, and a stitch or seal notch means accepted. Invalid,
stale, and blocked results retract previews, preserve prior state, and expose a
typed reason. Focus, description, reduced-motion, silent, and non-pointer forms
carry the same meanings while the domain remains authoritative.

Rationale:

- Current objects share `interactive-target` and glow treatment without a
  consistent visible interaction-state vocabulary.
- A common shell lets route-specific glyphs, words, layers, artifacts, and
  symbols retain identity while remaining operable through every input path.
- Typed rejection prevents decorative response from implying accepted state.

### 339. Shared Portal Visual Grammar

Selection: every route portal uses one nested frame-and-aperture silhouette
over its local material. A labeled incomplete inner ring exposes missing
requirements, eligibility closes the ring and adds one entry chevron, and focus
adds shared brackets plus explicit `Enter`. Only an accepted domain action may
start the inward paper-fold transition. Persisted completion adds one stable
stitch or seal notch. Non-color, reduced-motion, silent, and non-spatial forms
preserve meaning while semantic portal volumes remain separate from art.

Rationale:

- Current source mixes a generic portal ring, frame, gate, and
  collectible-shaped goal without a common state language.
- Nested frame and aperture forms connect the picture-frame fantasy to all
  eight route materials without copying an unsourced finale.
- Explicit domain acceptance prevents camera, device, or decorative crossing
  from completing a route.

### 340. Shared Lighting and Contrast Grammar

Selection: all routes use three semantic lighting layers: a neutral readability
field for JR and critical edges, one bounded route-landmark pool for palette and
depth, and state light emitted only after accepted domain events. Critical
shapes, textures, outlines, and labels remain readable with effects disabled.
Exposure, bloom, shadow contrast, and pulse area stay clamped; camera pixels and
room brightness never own state. High-contrast, static, reduced-effect, silent,
and low-tier forms drop decorative accents and shadows before critical cues.

Rationale:

- Current recipes reuse route-level `base`, `accent`, `glow`, and `danger`
  values across object roles, making glow alone an ambiguous state signal.
- Page 07 needs a light-reveal fantasy without giving sensing or exposure
  presentation semantic authority.
- Tier degradation and accessibility must preserve gameplay meaning before
  decoration.
