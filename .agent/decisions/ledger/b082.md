# Picture-Frame Platformer Decision Ledger

## Batch 082

Questions 406-410 received no user selection. These choices define the first
walking bay's source boundary, critical silhouette dependency, backing
construction, donor timing, and surface-work gate. They remain open to direct
user override and grant no asset generation, runtime implementation,
rights-sensitive reuse, placeholder retirement, promotion, push, or deployment
authority.

### 406. Walking-Bay Asset Source Assignment

Selection: use a custom-critical, bounded-support split. Keep canonical JR,
hero-frame identity and opening, all gameplay-critical contacts and top edges,
hazard mechanisms and cues, conservation joins, and safe handoff original to
Lost Pages. Limit repo-owned procedural work to neutral structural support,
repetitive laminations, low-tier forms, and fallbacks. Limit cleared donors to
noncritical backing parts, surface details, and neutral dressing under explicit
part maps, rights, lineage, hashes, and independent semantic companions.

Rationale:

- Critical identity and gameplay remain authored around fixed semantics.
- Bounded support can reduce production cost without gaining route authority.
- Provenance and deterministic fallbacks prevent donor availability from
  moving contacts, sockets, frame identity, or milestone timing.

### 407. Critical Silhouette Authoring Order

Selection: establish one `JR-to-Contact Readability Spine` first. Lock
canonical JR's final-scale silhouette, semantic and collider relationship,
poses, and visible Jump arc, then author the route-ordered contacts, hazard
telegraphs, recovery marks, and handoff inside the fixed aperture. Outer-frame
finish, backing, bounded support, surfaces, microdetail, and dressing follow
without rescaling JR or moving semantic contacts.

Rationale:

- JR and contact readability set the candidate's actual playable proportions.
- Route-order authoring exposes clearance and next-landing failures early.
- Decorative asset dimensions cannot silently redefine gameplay.

### 408. Backing-Bay Construction

Selection: build one build-time `Deterministic Archive Backplane`. A versioned
repo-owned recipe generates neutral rear panels, rails, braces, laminations,
tier variants, and fallback around pinned semantic roots, sockets, bounds, and
clearances. Independent gameplay semantics and the separate O2 wall-contact
plane and support hull remain authoritative; donors attach only through
declared dressing sockets.

Rationale:

- Repeatable presentation support can be regenerated from accepted dimensions.
- Semantic assembly and physical placement remain separate authorities.
- Device tiers reduce neutral presentation without changing structure or route.

### 409. Walking-Bay Donor Intake Timing

Selection: use an `Early Gate, Late Bind` donor flow. Audit an exact staged
source for provenance, rights, integrity, budget, and technical suitability
early, but bind it only after fixed semantics declare one need-first
noncritical role. A passing part or region enters a versioned adaptation;
rejection or absence uses its declared custom or procedural fallback.

Rationale:

- Early rejection exposes risk without putting donors on the critical path.
- Late role binding prevents native dimensions and hierarchy from sizing play.
- Exact adaptation and fallback records keep replacement deterministic.

### 410. Silhouette-to-Surface Handoff

Selection: require one hash-bound `Silhouette Readiness Packet`. Pin the
candidate, prerequisites, semantics, assembly, JR-to-contact spine, independent
collision, hazards, recovery, handoff, aperture, frame clearance, backplane,
sources, tiers, fallbacks, placeholder classes, proof, risks, and
producer/consumer verdicts. Open only bounded downstream visual candidate work
after applicable criteria pass; any dependency change stales affected work.

Rationale:

- Surface work begins from one reproducible, accepted candidate state.
- Exact evidence prevents attractive but incompatible revisions from mixing.
- The gate cannot retire placeholders or grant production promotion.
