# Picture-Frame Platformer Decision Ledger

## Batch 069

Questions 341-345 received no user reply. These selections define the
provisional shared visual grammar for rewards, checkpoints, dressing, material
layers, and motion hierarchy. They remain open to direct user override and
grant no asset generation, runtime implementation, rights-sensitive reuse,
push, or deployment authority.

### 341. Shared Collectible and Reward Grammar

Selection: preserve every reward's unique route-authored silhouette, material,
symbol, and label while presenting it on one shared three-stitch folio tab with
a stable receipt notch. Show only allowed objective or empty-slot information
before earning. Persist the reward and completion receipt before revealing the
artifact, closing its stitch halo, or folding it into the satchel or ledger.
Revisits show a non-interactive embossed imprint, owned label, and receipt
status through equivalent visual, descriptive, static, silent, and non-spatial
forms without respawn or re-award.

Rationale:

- Current routes mix fragments, marks, notes, badges, a stabilizer, and a key
  behind generic glow and collectible behavior.
- Unique artifacts preserve story meaning while one receipt language makes
  durable ownership legible.
- Persist-before-presentation keeps reward delivery idempotent.

### 342. Shared Checkpoint Visual Grammar

Selection: integrate a non-interactive stitched bookmark into each checkpoint
landing without changing its safe top edge. A hollow tab, stable checkpoint id,
number, and label identify an upcoming save. Only a durable domain receipt
fills the tab, closes two side stitches, and permits the bounded paper-fold
acknowledgment. Restore shows the same filled id and return arrow before resume.
Static, reduced-effect, silent, high-contrast, descriptive, and non-spatial
forms preserve saved-versus-unsaved meaning while presentation remains
non-authoritative.

Rationale:

- Current source exposes generic objective status but no durable checkpoint
  marker or exact restore identity.
- The bookmark belongs to the living-page language without resembling a portal
  or reward.
- Receipt-gated feedback prevents animation or collision from claiming a save.

### 343. Decorative Dressing Boundary

Selection: reserve critical edges, brackets, touch texture, checkpoint
bookmarks, portal apertures, receipt notches, semantic symbols, labels, and
state-light forms for gameplay bindings. Dressing uses quieter incomplete
contours, lower focal contrast, and ambient-only motion outside declared
readability, clearance, and interaction masks. It has no semantic collision,
hit target, valid preview, or mutation authority. Optional grouped descriptions
identify meaningful scenery, and tiers remove dressing before critical assets.

Rationale:

- Rich mixed-media worlds need scenery without creating false affordances.
- Current generic shapes and glow do not clearly separate interactive from
  decorative objects.
- Optional dressing can degrade without changing play, accessibility, or
  semantic proof.

### 344. Shared Material Layering Contract

Selection: build critical art from three bounded layers: stable semantic ink
for edges, textures, symbols, labels, and state marks; one route-authored paper,
wood, gilt, graphite, glass, canvas, or relic substrate; and optional patina
that cannot enter critical masks or reduce contrast. Pinned route and
readability profiles select validated light, dark, static, and low-tier
variants. Camera color, room light, effects, and decoration may affect ambience
but never select semantic meaning.

Rationale:

- Eight distinct route families must preserve one learned gameplay language.
- A stable semantic layer prevents unique materials from baking incompatible
  meanings into critical art.
- Masked patina retains handcrafted variety without sacrificing contrast.

### 345. Shared Motion and Focal Hierarchy

Selection: keep critical meaning static-first. Deterministic platform and
hazard motion follows fixed semantic paths and phases. Accepted state changes
use bounded anticipate, act, and settle acknowledgment with one primary focal
cue per local region; JR and the next required cue outrank landmark reaction.
Ambient dressing moves more slowly outside critical masks and freezes first
under tier or pause. Reduced motion uses stable poses, outlines, labels, and
short crossfades. Animation acknowledges accepted state but never advances it.

Rationale:

- Current routes already combine several loops, jitter effects, pulses, bounces,
  unfolds, and portal expansions that can compete for attention.
- A shared hierarchy protects JR, hazards, and the next required action.
- Static-first and reduced-motion forms preserve meaning independently from
  animation timing.
