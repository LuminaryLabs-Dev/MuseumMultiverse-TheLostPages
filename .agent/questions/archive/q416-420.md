## Question 417: Hero-Frame Focal Hierarchy

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the frame's fragments and finish are provisionally defined,
but their visual rank is not. The current generic scene gives several objects
similar glow emphasis; the cohesive frame instead needs one stable hierarchy
that remains recognizable without competing with JR, hazards, or the next
landing.

Question: Which focal hierarchy should govern the cohesive hero frame?

1. Use one `Aperture-First Conservation Hierarchy`. In placement and Plan, let
   the unobstructed nested-paper aperture and complete route read first, with
   the dark-oak outer silhouette holding the mounted-object shape. Use the
   repaired-gilt upper-left cornice and blackened-iron lower-right clamp as one
   restrained diagonal identity pair; keep the brass catalog rail and olive
   folio hinge/spine as quieter secondary evidence. In Run, keep JR and the next
   required cue above every frame fragment by lowering local frame contrast,
   texture frequency, and reflection near critical masks. Keep fragments fixed
   and non-pulsing except for the already bounded persisted `Conservation
   Register` acknowledgments. Preserve this rank through non-color structure,
   C1/C2/C3, static forms, phone crops, and wall/tabletop hosts.
2. Make the worn-gilt upper-left cornice the dominant hero in every view with
   the brightest value, strongest detail, reflection, and state reaction,
   allowing the route and JR to read as smaller content inside its decorative
   frame.
3. Give oak, gilt, brass, olive folio, blackened iron, paper aperture, route,
   and JR equal contrast, texture density, edge strength, and local animation
   so the assembled object reads as one uniformly busy conservation collage.
4. Let camera angle, room brightness, route phase, and current effect
   dynamically choose whichever fragment becomes dominant, changing
   reflection, saturation, outline, glow, and motion without a pinned focal
   order.

## Question 418: Hero-Frame Repair Cohesion

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the typed `Conservation Join` family already defines how
archive modules appear connected, but the hero frame still needs a bounded
visible repair language across mismatched oak, gilt, brass, cloth, iron, and
paper. Those repairs must unify the object without resembling safe edges,
checkpoint stitches, interaction brackets, or portal eligibility marks.

Question: Which repair language should unify the hero-frame fragments?

1. Use one `Four-Motif Visible Repair Network` bound to exact accepted
   non-gameplay edges. Join repaired gilt and paper to oak with warm-paper
   dovetail keys beneath restrained areas of leaf loss; attach brass rail to
   wood with small oxidized pins and saddles; wrap olive folio connections with
   short paired stitches that stop outside the aperture and critical masks; and
   seat blackened-iron clamps on quiet waxed support shoes. Give every motif a
   coherent physical scale, repeated spacing, low focal contrast, bounded
   count, source lineage, tier/static equivalents, and presentation-only
   identity. Reserve the safe double lip, checkpoint bookmark, portal ring,
   interaction brackets, semantic labels, and hazard marks exclusively for
   gameplay. Persisted `Conservation Register` events may reveal or connect
   only predeclared repair segments and never create topology or collision.
2. Hide every transition beneath seamless refinishing so oak, gilt, brass,
   cloth, iron, and paper blend into one flawless continuous luxury frame with
   no visible evidence of conservation, adaptation, or assembled provenance.
3. Give every junction its own unrelated ornate repair—different stitches,
   fasteners, inlays, cracks, seals, labels, and animations—with equal contrast
   and no bounded motif family or reserved gameplay vocabulary.
4. Use procedural grime, edge wear, bloom, shadows, dust, and particles to
   visually blend adjacent fragments at runtime, sampling their nearby colors
   and geometry without explicit join identities, masks, sources, or static
   equivalents.

## Question 419: Hero-Frame Detail Scale Budget

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the archive grammar already classifies removable microdetail
below `0.25J` and requires runtime and key art to use the same accepted
revision, but the hero frame still needs an exact distance hierarchy. Detail
should reward closer inspection without shimmering on phones, overwhelming the
route, or becoming key-art-only fiction.

Question: Which detail-scale budget should govern the hero frame?

1. Use one `Three-Distance Detail Ladder`. Keep landmark-scale outer silhouette,
   aperture, major fragment breaks, and the gilt/iron identity diagonal
   readable in every tier and full-object view. Keep recognition-scale material
   borders, paper dovetail keys, brass pins, olive stitches, iron shoes, and
   stable labels at `0.25J-1J`, with authored simplifications that retain their
   motif in C1/C2. Confine fibers, pores, tiny scratches, leaf cracks, dust
   impressions, and other sub-`0.25J` microdetail to declared noncritical masks:
   C3 may retain the accepted bounded set, C2 merges or bakes it quietly, and C1
   removes it. Pin physical/texel scale, contrast and frequency ceilings,
   mip/compression variants, phone and host crops, and anti-shimmer proof.
   Runtime and key art render the same accepted tier revision; key art may use
   a closer approved camera but cannot add unseen geometry, materials, repairs,
   or wear.
2. Author the entire frame for extreme close-up first with dense fibers,
   scratches, cracks, labels, embossed marks, relief, and layered wear on every
   surface, then downsample those assets automatically for normal runtime views.
3. Apply one uniform texture density, edge frequency, repair complexity, and
   microdetail level across every fragment and tier so distant oak, small pins,
   the aperture, and close-up paper all receive equal visual information.
4. Generate scratches, dust, fibers, edge wear, leaf cracks, and patina
   procedurally per device, camera distance, and session, while letting key art
   use a separate hand-painted high-resolution frame revision with additional
   details not present in runtime.

## Question 420: Hero-Frame Cohesion Handoff

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: Questions 416-419 define the proposed first decorative
consumer, focal hierarchy, visible repair network, and distance-detail ladder.
The current generic visual dataset has no exact frame-pass boundary, so the
backing and dressing consumer needs one accepted frame revision and protected
attachment envelope rather than an inferred visual state.

Question: What should close the hero-frame pass and unlock backing and
dressing?

1. Require one hash-bound `Hero-Frame Cohesion Packet`. Pin the prerequisite
   `Critical Surface Acceptance Packet`; frame root, silhouette, `3:2`
   aperture, wall contact, opening, gameplay clearance, critical masks, and
   cameras; exact fragment, model, material, source, adaptation, and output
   hashes; the accepted focal hierarchy, repair network, and distance-detail
   ladder; C1/C2/C3 and accessible static forms; and placement/Plan/Run phone,
   physical, wall/tabletop, browser/human-view, performance, fallback, risk,
   and producer/consumer verdicts. Unlock only the `Deterministic Archive
   Backplane` and one bounded dressing consumer through declared semantic root,
   attachment sockets, rear-depth envelope, masks, budgets, and fallbacks.
   Relevant dependency changes stale downstream evidence. The packet grants no
   critical-mask, semantic, gameplay, placeholder-retirement, promotion, push,
   or deployment authority.
2. Share the latest frame GLB, materials, and screenshots in a folder and let
   the backing and dressing team infer the current root, openings, attachment
   points, protected regions, depth, tier, and fallback constraints.
3. Approve the frame from one polished full-frame or key-art beauty render,
   then treat that image as sufficient authority for backing, dressing,
   placement, tier, accessibility, and performance work.
4. Start backing and dressing in parallel before the frame evidence is
   complete, let both consumers choose provisional sockets and depth, and
   reconcile overlaps, masks, and revisions after all three passes look
   finished.
