# Picture-Frame Platformer Interview: Questions 396-400

## Question 396: Page 05 Archive Module Inventory

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the current Page 05 app still renders a generic avatar, three
generic hazards, and a gate, while the provisional design requires one
recomposable impossible-archive kit. A fixed first inventory is needed to
bound custom modeling, donor adaptation, procedural support, socket placement,
variant reuse, budgets, accessibility, and proof.

Question: How broad should Page 05's first production archive-module inventory
be?

1. Build one fifteen-module `Archive Core`. Use three shared structural
   modules—backing bay, safe landing, and checkpoint saddle; four paper/book
   modules—folded-page ledge, stitched bridge, book-spine stair, and
   living-paper ramp; four furniture/catalog modules—shelf span, drawer lift,
   catalog-rail bridge, and label shutter; and four frame/artifact
   modules—nested-frame gate, easel brace, gilt ledge, and relic plinth. Give
   each stable visual identity, canonical pivot and bounds, typed sockets,
   material and tier bindings, fallback, provenance, and an independent
   semantic companion. Recompose the same accepted modules across all three
   variants. Keep critical silhouettes and active mechanisms custom Lost Pages
   work; permit cleared donors only for bounded noncritical surfaces, details,
   or supports and deterministic procedural forms only for backing, braces,
   and dressing. Keep JR, hazards, discoveries, and the final portal in their
   existing separate catalogs.
2. Build one six-module lean kit and repeatedly rescale, recolor, and relabel
   the same ledge, bridge, stair, shelf, frame, and plinth across every section
   and variant.
3. Build at least forty-five bespoke modules, giving every variant its own
   unique structural, paper, furniture, frame, and artifact set with little or
   no cross-variant reuse.
4. Keep an open pool of staged assets and procedural shapes, choosing and
   assembling whatever visually fits during builds or runtime without one
   closed inventory, stable module identities, or fixed source boundaries.

## Question 397: Page 05 Archive Module Scale Grammar

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the `3:2` aperture, JR height, lane depths, and whole-course
uniform scaling are provisionally fixed, but individual archive modules still
have no common size language. Without one, the same book, shelf, frame, or
artifact could change perceived scale, jump readability, socket fit, and
physical viewing comfort between variants or devices.

Question: How should the fifteen proposed archive modules be sized inside the
Page 05 aperture?

1. Use one canonical JR-relative scale grammar. In authoring space set aperture
   width `W = 1`, height `H = 2W/3`, and canonical JR height
   `J = 0.135H = 0.09W`. Classify removable microdetail below `0.25J`;
   character-scale labels, handles, stitches, and relic details from
   `0.25J-1J`; traversal modules from `1J-3.5J` in their dominant span; and
   structural bay or landmark modules from `3.5J-8J`, never exceeding the
   approved aperture margins or rear/front depth envelopes. Pin exact canonical
   bounds, pivot, support/contact surfaces, sockets, clearance, semantic
   companion, and intended band per module revision. Use unit scale for every
   recipe instance; only the already validated uniform course root may convert
   canonical space to wall or tabletop meters. Normalize source donors through
   explicit adapters, reject out-of-band or nonuniform fit, and require LOD,
   low-tier, static, and accessibility variants to preserve silhouette,
   contact, labels, and critical edge thickness at `1.5-2.5 m`.
2. Size books, labels, shelves, frames, and artifacts at realistic museum
   dimensions in meters, even when their relationship to JR, the aperture, Jump
   geometry, and cross-variant composition changes.
3. Let each variant freely rescale every module, including nonuniform scaling,
   until its composition looks balanced, storing only the final transforms
   without a shared size band or source-adapter rule.
4. Change module size dynamically to maintain a preferred screen-space pixel
   height as the device, viewing distance, camera crop, wall fit, or viewport
   changes.

## Question 398: Page 05 Module Pivot and Orientation

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the frame already uses canonical `+X` right, `+Y` up, and
`+Z` toward the player, but the proposed modules do not yet have a local-origin
rule. Stable functional pivots are required for deterministic socket placement,
support contact, animation axes, donor adaptation, revision comparison, and
preventing visual correction offsets from changing route geometry.

Question: Which pivot and local-orientation convention should govern Page 05's
archive modules?

1. Use one typed `Functional Origin` contract. Author every module in a
   right-handed default pose aligned to frame-local `+X/+Y/+Z`. Put a traversal
   module's semantic origin at its primary entry-support center with local
   `+X` toward its authored exit; put a static structural module's origin at
   its primary parent-mount or support-contact centroid; put a hinged, sliding,
   or rotating mechanism's origin on its authoritative motion axis at the
   accepted rest pose; and put a mounted dressing module's origin at its
   declared mount contact. Declare all other support surfaces, contacts,
   sockets, animation paths, bounds, and clearance relative to that origin.
   A stable module revision pins its typed origin, normalized quaternion, and
   named finite symmetry set; changing any of them is a semantic revision.
   Adapt imported assets once through an explicit native-to-canonical transform,
   ignore mesh centers and source pivots as authority, and forbid post-solve
   offsets, camera-facing reorientation, arbitrary mirroring, or per-variant
   correction transforms.
2. Center every module's origin on its rendered axis-aligned bounding box and
   align local axes to the current mesh bounds, regardless of support, travel,
   hinge, or mount function.
3. Preserve whatever pivot, axis orientation, node hierarchy, and parent
   transforms arrive in each GLB or DCC export and place semantic sockets around
   those imported values.
4. Let each variant or runtime placement visually recenter, rotate, mirror, and
   offset modules after socket assembly until contacts and composition appear
   correct from the current camera.

## Question 399: Page 05 Module Connection Appearance

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: Page 05 already has typed semantic sockets, deterministic
transforms, contact limits, and collider-seam validation, but the visible join
between accepted modules is undefined. The join language must make the
impossible archive feel deliberately conserved while preserving continuous
route edges and keeping visual braces outside transform authority.

Question: How should accepted archive-module connections look inside Page 05?

1. Use one typed `Conservation Join` family over the accepted semantic edge.
   Join paper and book modules with a shallow overlapping paper tongue plus a
   paired stitch or fold score; join wood and catalog furniture with a
   restrained dovetail or tenon shadow plus one brass registration pin; and
   join frame or artifact modules with a nested-paper repair key, blackened
   clamp, or support saddle selected by the closed connection type. Bind one
   stable join descriptor to the exact parent socket, child socket, edge id,
   material masks, canonical physical size, clearance mask, tier forms, and
   fallback. Required traversal joins preserve one continuous top silhouette,
   semantic collider seam, non-color edge treatment, and directional cue.
   Visible braces and repair marks may validate or present the accepted join
   but never move endpoints, infer topology, cover JR or the next cue, or
   acknowledge gameplay. Static, high-contrast, and low-tier forms preserve a
   stable notch, stitch, pin, or clamp silhouette without particles or glow.
2. Hide every joint by intersecting and blending the meshes into a visually
   seamless continuous surface, even when the material families and support
   directions differ.
3. Place the same bright glowing ring, bolt, or holographic connector at every
   paper, wood, furniture, frame, and artifact socket so all joins share one
   obvious effect.
4. Procedurally weld, stretch, bevel, or grow material between nearby meshes at
   runtime, selecting a connector from geometry and proximity after assembly
   rather than from the pinned semantic edge.

## Question 400: Page 05 Section Composition Density

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the proposed inventory bounds module types, but not how many
instances may share one checkpoint section. A fixed composition budget is
needed to keep JR, routes, layered depth, hazards, joins, and the portal
readable while honoring mobile performance tiers without changing gameplay.

Question: How densely should Page 05 compose archive modules in each section?

1. Use one progressive `Readable Bay` budget. Permit at most six archive-module
   instances in Section 1, eight in Section 2, and ten in Section 3, excluding
   canonical JR, the separately cataloged hazards and discoveries, and the
   final portal. Keep those accepted semantic instances identical across C1,
   C2, and C3 and pin them in the route recipe. Allow at most one neutral
   dressing instance per section on C1, two on C2, and four on C3; dressing
   remains outside required paths, critical masks, sockets, and state. Within
   every view, retain one primary focal mechanism, the existing maximum of two
   simultaneous active hazards, and unobstructed reads of JR, the next landing,
   next hazard or transition cue, and goal direction. De-emphasize deeper or
   future modules through static contrast and depth treatment rather than
   hiding semantic content. Validate full-frame Plan and Run compositions at
   standard/seated distance, `0°/30°`, every tier, high contrast, reduced
   motion, static presentation, and forced dressing fallback.
2. Keep every section equally sparse with at most five archive-module
   instances and one dressing object, even when Section 3 must show three lanes
   and the combined portal challenge.
3. Allow up to twelve module instances in Section 1, eighteen in Section 2,
   twenty-four in Section 3, and twelve additional dressing objects per
   section to emphasize archive density.
4. Add and remove structural, traversal, mechanism, and dressing modules
   dynamically according to device performance, viewing angle, wall size,
   camera crop, and current frame rate, allowing the visible route composition
   to differ during play.
