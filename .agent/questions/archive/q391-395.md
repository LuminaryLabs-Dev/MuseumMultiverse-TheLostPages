## Question 391: Page 05 Impossible Frame Fragment Composition

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the hero frame is already provisionally an original stable
assemblage of mismatched museum eras, but its exact first-read silhouette and
fragment hierarchy are unresolved. A defined composition is needed before a
cleared structural donor, custom modeling, materials, milestone reactions, and
key-art framing can be judged against one coherent Lost Pages identity.

Question: Which fragment composition should define Page 05's stable impossible
hero frame?

1. Build one original `Conservator's Impossible Portal`. Use a custom dark-oak
   rectangular structural core around a large unobstructed paper aperture, then
   balance four fixed asymmetric fragment families: a broken aged-gilt cornice
   at upper left repaired with visible paper dovetails; a narrow brass catalog
   rail and label-tab sockets down the left and lower edge; an olive
   book-cloth folio hinge and stitched spine at upper right; and a blackened
   iron conservation clamp and support shoe at lower right. Unite them with a
   shallow nested-paper inner frame, restrained era seams, and stable original
   Lost Pages marks. A passing `center-frame-set.glb` may donate only bounded
   structural pieces behind this authored composition. Keep the opening, JR,
   route, portal direction, and goal clear from every approved viewing
   envelope; fragments remain presentation-only and non-collidable. Teal,
   olive, and gold milestones travel through fixed inlay channels, labels, and
   non-color repair marks without changing the outer silhouette. Provide
   static, reduced-motion, silent, described, high-contrast, and low-tier
   equivalents.
2. Divide the frame evenly into eight equally prominent museum-era segments,
   each with its own ornament, relic, label, material, animation, and route
   reference around the entire opening.
3. Use one symmetrical restored ornate gilt frame with matching carved corners,
   conventional museum molding, and only palette or glow changes between
   sections.
4. Randomly replace, rotate, resize, and rearrange sourced frame fragments for
   each route variant and replay, allowing the outer silhouette, opening,
   materials, focal hierarchy, and key-art composition to change by seed.

## Question 392: Page 05 Playable Aperture Proportion

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: Page 05 already has canonical depth limits and JR scale relative
to the opening, but the opening's width-to-height ratio is unspecified. That
ratio controls horizontal route readability, safe Jump framing, wall fit,
uniform physical scale, accessibility composition, fallback parity, and the
camera envelope used by runtime and key art.

Question: What fixed proportion should define Page 05's playable picture
aperture?

1. Use one `3:2` landscape aperture. Author canonical gameplay with opening
   width `W` and height `H = 2W/3`, preserve the existing JR-height and
   front/rear-depth rules relative to that aperture, and reserve stable inner
   edge margins for critical cues without shrinking semantic space at runtime.
   Keep the dark-oak core and asymmetric fragments outside one fixed opening
   clearance envelope. Wall placement and tabletop fallback choose only one
   validated uniform root scale; neither host nor device may reshape or reflow
   the course. Pin full-frame and route cameras, crop-safe regions, standard and
   seated viewing envelopes, static/accessibility layouts, and physical-device
   proof to the same ratio. Reject a placement that cannot fit rather than
   changing aspect or gameplay coordinates.
2. Use a wide `16:9` aperture to maximize horizontal travel, accepting a
   shorter JR and landmark read on portrait phones and requiring more physical
   wall width.
3. Use a near-square `1:1` aperture to emphasize a compact museum object,
   compressing horizontal course preview and increasing vertical stacking.
4. Let the aperture continuously match the phone viewport or detected wall,
   reflowing platforms, hazards, lanes, camera framing, cue margins, Jump
   relationships, and key art between portrait, square, and landscape shapes.

## Question 393: Page 05 Canvas-to-Course Opening

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: Page 05 broadly turns a flat canvas into layered paper
architecture, but its first opening beat is not yet defined. The reveal must
make the picture-frame fantasy legible without concealing content preparation,
moving the validated camera, granting gameplay state, or leaving an
inaccessible half-open scene after interruption.

Question: How should Page 05's closed canvas become the first playable lane?

1. Use one prepared four-leaf unfold. After `Confirm Frame` persists placement
   and the exact route package and bindings pass preflight, hold a closed
   canvas with one static repair mark and contextual `Open Lost Page` hero.
   Acceptance starts a fixed-camera, skippable 1.8-second presentation-only
   sequence: register the repair mark and section title; release at most four
   pre-seamed leaves inward from the edges; separate the painted paper into
   shallow front, middle, and rear layers; fold the lower leaf into the first
   safe landing while side leaves settle as margins; then place canonical JR
   on the safe start perch and hand control to the existing Plan hero. Perform
   no network, storage, decode, compile, semantic mutation, progression grant,
   or camera authority during the reveal. Provide an immediate skip to the
   exact settled state, a brief crossfade and ordered description for reduced
   motion, an immediate illustrated final state for static presentation, and
   low-tier parity that removes only secondary folds and particles. An
   interruption restores either the prepared closed canvas or the exact
   settled course, never a half-open state.
2. Open the canvas automatically as soon as placement locks, allowing the fold
   to run while route content, bindings, and materials continue loading and
   without a separate ready or skip control.
3. Fly the camera through the canvas into the miniature world, using a long
   cinematic move to reveal depth before returning control at the first
   platform.
4. Tear or dissolve the canvas into particles, mist, or loose scraps and
   reconstruct the course dynamically on each entry without a stable
   leaf-to-layer relationship or equivalent static composition.

## Question 394: Page 05 Milestone Frame Reactions

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the frame is already provisionally stable during traversal and

fragments. A fixed reaction language can make course progress readable without
resembling tracking drift, obscuring the aperture, or letting animation decide
gameplay state.

Question: How should Page 05's fixed hero frame react after the course opens?

1. Use one bounded `Conservation Register` sequence driven only by acknowledged
   semantic milestones. At the first checkpoint, advance one brass catalog tab
   and expose an olive stitched notch along the left rail. At the second, close
   the lower-right conservation clamp by one shallow presentation-only step and
   connect the repaired upper-left paper dovetails with a gold line. At portal
   readiness, let the nested-paper inner frame make one restrained inward and
   outward pulse while four non-color corner registration marks resolve. On
   durable completion, settle the teal, olive, and gold inlays into one
   continuous repaired border and reveal the final Page 05 label. Keep every
   reaction local, deterministic, under 700 milliseconds, non-collidable, and
   outside the aperture; never translate, rotate, scale, flex, or rearrange the
   frame root or outer silhouette. Persist gameplay before presentation, and
   let neither animation completion nor presentation acknowledgment grant
   progress. Reduced motion crossfades directly to each exact registered state;
   static, silent, described, high-contrast, and low-tier forms preserve the
   tabs, stitches, corner marks, label, and ordered state names.
2. Give every checkpoint, portal-ready state, failure, Jump, layer change, and
   collectible the same whole-frame glow and breathing pulse, varying only
   color and intensity.
3. Continuously flex the frame, shake its fragments near hazards, and move the
   molding in response to JR's position, Jump arc, and live layer dragging.
4. Reassemble the frame at every section by rotating, translating, replacing,
   and resizing its fragments, allowing the silhouette, aperture clearance,
   wall contact, and viewing composition to change with progress.

## Question 395: Page 05 Hero-Frame Material Finish

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the shared three-layer material contract preserves gameplay
readability, but Page 05 still needs one authored finish that unifies its
mismatched oak, gilt, brass, cloth, iron, and paper fragments. This determines
texture production, shader scope, lighting response, aging, device tiers,
source adaptation, and the frame's tactile first read.

Question: Which material finish should unify Page 05's impossible hero frame?

1. Use one restrained `Handled Conservation Object` finish. Give the dark-oak
   core a matte open grain; the upper-left cornice muted worn leaf over visible
   red-brown bole and paper-dovetail repairs; the catalog rail oxidized brass
   with only bounded contact-polished edges; the folio hinge woven olive book
   cloth with legible thread and repaired fray; the clamp waxed blackened iron
   with sparse edge wear; and the inner aperture warm rag paper with stable ink
   registration. Unify every substrate through the existing semantic-ink,
   authored-substrate, and mask-safe-patina layers, one coherent physical
   microtexture scale, restrained saturation, repeated repair seams, and
   authored frame-local key and rim light with clamped neutral room ambience.
   Pin light/dark, high-contrast, static, and low-tier variants; remove
   secondary fibers, micro-scratches, and specular detail first while
   preserving silhouette, labels, non-color state marks, material family, and
   critical contrast. Forbid live camera reflections, environment-derived
   color meaning, unbounded procedural wear, animated texture authority, and
   source pixels without rights and transformation lineage.
2. Use photoreal museum-object materials with high-resolution scans,
   fingerprints, scratches, ray-traced reflections, dynamic room-color
   sampling, and procedural grime variation as the main source of realism and
   state readability.
3. Flatten every fragment into the same cream paper-and-black-ink cutout
   material, representing oak, gilt, brass, cloth, and iron only through
   printed labels and silhouette.
4. Turn every fragment into a luminous fantasy material with flowing emission,
   animated iridescence, color-shifting metal, and portal glow that changes
   continuously during traversal.
