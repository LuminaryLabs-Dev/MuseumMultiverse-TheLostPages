# Picture-Frame Platformer Interview: Questions 381-385

## Question 381: Mixed-Media Runtime Construction

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the current app uses generic JavaScript shape and color
descriptors plus launcher placeholder images. The approved mixed-media
direction needs a concrete runtime construction method that remains readable
and performant on phones, lets key art come from the playable scene, and never
lets renderer meshes or pixels become gameplay authority.

Question: How should the handcrafted Lost Pages worlds be constructed at
runtime?

1. Use one hybrid modular asset kit. Build gameplay-critical platforms,
   hazards, glyphs, and route marks from lightweight shallow-extruded or layered
   paper modules with approved texture atlases and stable visual bindings over
   independent semantic volumes. Reserve compact custom glTF modules for hero
   landmarks, signature mechanisms, rewards, and selected dimensional props;
   keep JR as the canonical hybrid 2.5D cutout rig. Share restrained paper,
   ink, cloth, gilt, and edge-material families while each route binds its own
   palette and motif set. Render launcher plates from the same accepted scene.
   Device tiers may remove dressing, secondary motion, texture resolution, and
   optional depth only; they preserve every critical silhouette, cue, route,
   collision, and accessible equivalent. Require fixed packaged assets and
   prohibit runtime network art generation or pixel-derived gameplay.
2. Build every route from unique high-poly photoreal glTF rooms, props,
   characters, and physically based materials, then derive collision and
   interaction directly from the final visible meshes.
3. Flatten every landmark, platform, character, and effect into camera-facing
   image billboards with no shallow construction, true depth, material edges,
   dimensional signature props, or route-specific spatial identity.
4. Generate textures, characters, and meshes from prompts during each session,
   allowing model output, downloaded images, inferred bounds, and visual pixels
   to determine route geometry, hazards, interaction targets, and key art.

## Question 382: Material and Texture Catalog

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: current routes expose mostly color-only material descriptors,
while the launcher separately builds procedural paper shaders and canvas
textures. A finished hybrid asset kit needs one deterministic catalog that
keeps route identity, phone budgets, accessible cues, provenance, and key-art
parity intact without turning textures or shaders into gameplay authority.

Question: How should paper, cloth, ink, wood, and gilt materials be packaged
across all eight routes?

1. Use one versioned two-level material catalog. Keep a compact shared core of
   approved paper fiber, ink edge, book cloth, aged wood, gilt, and
   non-color-cue masks plus bounded deterministic shader profiles; give each
   route one small palette-and-motif atlas or array layer. Bind every material
   through a stable id, revision, content hash, source and rights record, color
   space, channel layout, UV contract, supported resolution tiers, semantic
   display role, and approved fallback. Keep critical outlines, hatches,
   symbols, and interaction marks in dedicated masks, shallow geometry, or
   equivalent text so compression, color loss, and lower tiers cannot erase
   meaning. Prepackage compressed mipmapped variants, use the exact accepted
   revisions for runtime and key art, and let tiers reduce resolution or
   decorative microdetail only. Shaders and textures never define collision,
   matching, progress, or eligibility.
2. Pack every route and state into one monolithic maximum-resolution atlas to
   minimize bindings, accepting that one material revision invalidates the
   entire journey and that all phones must load the complete texture set.
3. Let each mesh and artist carry arbitrary independent physically based
   textures, shader graphs, color spaces, UV conventions, resolution, naming,
   and fallbacks without a shared catalog or route-level budget.
4. Stream or generate materials during play from remote prompts and camera
   samples, allowing downloaded pixels, inferred palettes, and live shader
   output to change visual cues, key art, route identity, and asset bounds.

## Question 383: Route Asset Packaging

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the current launcher embeds placeholder JPEG data in source,
the book loads one GLB directly, and routes expose local JavaScript
descriptors. The mixed-media build needs a cloud-friendly package boundary
that keeps eight routes coherent without forcing every phone to download every
world or allowing required art to arrive unpredictably during play.

Question: How should approved runtime art be divided into downloadable and
cacheable packages?

1. Publish one content-addressed package graph beneath an atomic all-eight
   release root. Keep a small shared-core package for canonical JR, shared
   critical cue carriers, common materials, and renderer bindings; publish one
   tiered route package per experience for its hero landmark, signature
   mechanisms, route atlas, animations, required props, and approved
   fallbacks; and publish one tiny journey-motif and reward package for the
   launcher, receipts, restored overview, and Page 08 sockets so those surfaces
   never load seven complete prior worlds. Keep reproducible key-art masters
   and crops in a separate source-bound presentation catalog. Every manifest
   pins ids, revisions, hashes, byte sizes, dependencies, supported tiers,
   provenance and rights, critical versus dressing classification, and
   fallback bindings. Before reveal, preflight and cache the exact core, route,
   tier, and required dependencies; only declared neutral dressing may stream
   later. Permit route-local fetch, failure, quarantine, and eviction while the
   accepted all-eight root preserves release coherence and rollback.
2. Ship one monolithic maximum-detail bundle containing all eight playable
   worlds, every texture tier, animation, key-art derivative, and optional prop
   before the launcher can open.
3. Publish every mesh, texture, material, animation, and cue as an independent
   loose file and fetch it on demand during play without a route manifest,
   complete preflight, coherent revision root, or required-versus-dressing
   boundary.
4. Continue embedding images, models, and binary strings directly inside route
   JavaScript modules and page-texture source, using filenames and import order
   instead of versioned manifests, content hashes, provenance, cache leases,
   or atomic release identity.

## Question 384: Landmark Animation and VFX Authority

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: JR already has a provisional fixed nine-action animation
contract, but current route files name loose effects such as `ring-expand`,
`jitter-fade`, and `shake-flash` with millisecond durations. The finished
landmarks need expressive route-specific motion without allowing animation
callbacks, particles, physics, or hidden timelines to decide gameplay state.

Question: How should non-JR landmark animation and visual effects be authored
and triggered?

1. Use one closed typed presentation catalog driven only by stable semantic
   state and event ids. Share a small intent vocabulary for
   `dormant`, `focus`, `accepted`, `rejected`, `checkpoint`, `goal-ready`,
   `complete`, `blocked`, and `recovery`, while each route package binds
   original landmark-specific clips, poses, materials, particles, and static
   frames to those intents. Pin every binding's id, revision, hash, source,
   duration or bounded loop, interruption and pause rule, performance tier,
   focal envelope, reduced-motion frame, silent and screen-reader meaning, and
   completion fallback. Persist semantic state before any milestone
   presentation; use a stable acknowledgment only to release a presentation
   pause, never to grant progress. Animation callbacks, final mesh poses,
   particles, cloth or rigid-body simulation, elapsed render time, and effect
   success cannot change collision, matching, checkpoints, rewards, or
   eligibility. Package and preflight every required clip with its route;
   tiers remove secondary motion and particles first while static
   silhouettes, labels, and state meaning remain complete.
2. Let every route author an unrestricted timeline with arbitrary clip names,
   durations, loops, camera moves, and animation-complete callbacks that
   advance objectives and award progress.
3. Drive landmark changes primarily through live cloth, rigid-body, particle,
   and shader simulation, using whichever final pose or brightness threshold
   appears on the device to determine route state and collision.
4. Replace state-bound landmark motion with full-screen prerecorded videos
   that temporarily cover the AR scene, controls, and cues and must finish
   before progress can save or play can resume.

## Question 385: Deterministic Visual Review Surface

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the proof ladder already assigns promotion authority to O5,
but the current app has no single candidate-review surface that combines exact
route state, 3D placement, asset bindings, phone tiers, accessibility modes,
key-art cameras, and diagnostic overlays before promotion. Reviewers need
shared evidence without turning an editor, screenshot, or beauty render into
gameplay or approval authority.

Question: How should artists and reviewers inspect a route-art candidate before
promotion?

1. Build one repo-owned deterministic read-only `Visual Review Lab` that loads
   the exact content-addressed candidate manifest and accepted semantic
   snapshot. Let reviewers select route and authored variant, checkpoint or
   landmark state, wall/tabletop/floor-pedestal host profile, validated
   distance and angle, device tier, standard/high-contrast/reduced-motion/static
   presentation, and canonical key-art camera without editing the candidate.
   Keep the normal first view focused on the player-facing scene; disclose
   diagnostic overlays for semantic volumes, visual bounds, pivots, anchors,
   sockets, support/contact planes, safe viewing zone, focal envelope, cue
   contrast, animation intent, asset ids and hashes, budgets, and fallback
   state. Include side-by-side runtime/key-art crops, forced missing or corrupt
   binding recovery, and reproducible still or trace capture. Export one
   immutable review packet bound to candidate, build, recipe, camera, tier,
   preference, and evidence hashes. The lab may flag issues but cannot change
   semantics, repair art, accept rights, waive gates, promote, deploy, or
   replace browser, phone, physical-AR, and human-view proof.
2. Treat the artist's DCC viewport and beauty render as final proof, without
   loading the actual route recipe, runtime renderer, physical host profiles,
   device tiers, accessibility states, recovery paths, or phone composition.
3. Review only after deploying the candidate to the public production URL,
   letting live users discover binding, placement, accessibility, performance,
   and key-art mismatches.
4. Exchange manually named screenshots and screen recordings in chat without
   exact candidate hashes, repeatable cameras, semantic overlays, forced
   failures, evidence metadata, or a distinction between review and approval.
