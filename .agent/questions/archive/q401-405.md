## Question 402: Folded Stacks Walking-Bay Route

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: Question 401 leaves the first Page 05 production slice open,
but its recommended walking bay still names modules and hazards without fixing
one player-readable route. The route composition determines module edges,
hazard order, recovery anchors, camera proof, silhouette hierarchy, and whether
the picture-frame platforming fantasy replaces the current generic hazard line.

Question: Which route composition should define the proposed six-module
`Folded Stacks Walking Bay`?

1. Use one authored left-to-right rising read. Root the backing bay behind the
   complete route; open on the lower-left safe landing; teach the first
   forgiving Jump across a clearly telegraphed folding-page ledge; settle on a
   short stitched bridge; climb a book-spine stair that previews the next
   hazard; cross one tearing gap; and rise on the living-paper ramp into the
   upper-right safe section handoff. Keep one lane, no manipulation, no camera
   pan, and the next required landing visible. A miss before the bridge returns
   to the safe start, while a later miss returns to the bridge recovery anchor.
   Bind every hazard to independent semantics and keep the backing bay
   presentation-only outside its declared structural role.
2. Arrange the six modules as one central obstacle tableau with JR entering
   from below, both hazards active around the center, several equally prominent
   exits, and no fixed first-read traversal direction.
3. Stack the six modules into a steep vertical switchback that requires the
   camera to pan upward, hides later landings until JR climbs, and alternates
   leftward and rightward jumps around the frame center.
4. Shuffle module order, attachment edges, hazard positions, recovery anchors,
   and handoff height from a valid set on each entry so the first section plays
   differently across runs and devices.

## Question 403: Walking-Bay Viewing Composition

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: a readable route can still lose the picture-frame fantasy if
the playable view crops the frame away, while an always-distant full-object
view can make JR and hazards too small on a phone. The chosen composition must
govern physical viewing guidance, desktop fallback, proof cameras, and
truthful key art without giving presentation control over gameplay.

Question: Which viewing composition should govern the proposed
`Folded Stacks Walking Bay`?

1. Use one frame-anchored dual envelope. Placement and Plan show the entire
   outer frame, wall contact, opening, and clear margin. Run permits a closer
   guided physical view that still keeps the complete `3:2` aperture, full
   six-module route, both required hazards, goal direction, and at least two
   signature outer-frame fragments visible while JR and critical cues pass
   phone readability limits. Keep the world locked; optional viewer movement
   creates parallax but never changes the route or becomes required. Recenter
   guidance responds to drift rather than moving the world or snapping the
   device camera. Pin equivalent desktop, tabletop-fallback, reduced-motion,
   static, proof, and key-art compositions.
2. Keep the entire outer frame, wall margin, and nearby wall visible throughout
   Run on every device, accepting a smaller JR, shorter cue strokes, and less
   route detail on phone screens.
3. Crop tightly to a flat side-on course view during Run so JR, platforms, and
   hazards fill the screen, allowing nearly all outer-frame fragments, wall
   contact, aperture depth, and physical parallax to disappear.
4. Use a dynamic JR-following camera with automatic pans and zooms, then require
   the player to orbit or move closer to reveal hidden landings and hazards as
   part of completing the route.

## Question 404: Walking-Bay Depth Staging

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: Page 05 already has canonical front, middle, and back lane
depths, but the first one-lane bay has not been assigned to a slot or given a
surrounding 3D staging rule. That choice determines parallax, occlusion,
module sockets, collision placement, visual hierarchy, and how later sections
introduce deeper lanes.

Question: How should the proposed `Folded Stacks Walking Bay` use the picture
frame's depth?

1. Put every Section 1 traversal surface, JR path, and hazard on the canonical
   front lane at `-0.04W`. Keep the backing bay and bounded archive dressing
   behind the route on declared non-traversal mounts, with shallow paper layers
   establishing parallax without pretending to be landings. Permit only the
   prepared canvas-leaf margins and already-approved noninteractive effects in
   front of the canvas, with critical-view occlusion rules intact. Add the
   middle lane in Section 2 and the back lane in Section 3 so depth complexity
   grows with the teaching arc. The structural root never becomes a playable
   lane and presentation geometry never owns collision.
2. Put the complete Section 1 route on the canonical middle lane at `-0.10W`,
   surrounding it with equally prominent foreground and background paper,
   furniture, and frame fragments before any depth transition is taught.
3. Put the complete route on the back lane at `-0.16W` to emphasize a deep
   shadow-box world, accepting more foreground overlap, smaller projected
   gameplay forms, and greater reliance on cutaways and close viewing.
4. Spread the six visual modules across all three lane depths while keeping
   their semantic collision on one hidden plane, allowing perspective to imply
   transitions and contacts that the route graph does not contain.
