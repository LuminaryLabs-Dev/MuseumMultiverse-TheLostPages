# Picture-Frame Platformer Interview: Questions 406-410

## Question 406: Walking-Bay Asset Source Assignment

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: Batch 081 now provisionally selects one integrated semantic
silhouette candidate, while the broader asset contract permits original,
cleared donor, and deterministic procedural sources under different roles. The
first bay still needs an exact source boundary so available assets cannot
silently define its frame identity, traversal contacts, hazard behavior, or
goal read.

Question: Which source assignment should govern the proposed
`Folded Stacks Walking Bay`?

1. Use a custom-critical, bounded-support split. Keep canonical JR, the visible
   hero-frame composition and opening, every gameplay-critical landing and
   top-edge silhouette, folding and tearing hazard mechanisms and cues,
   conservation-join identity, and safe-handoff read as original Lost Pages
   work. Permit deterministic repo-owned procedural construction only for
   hidden backing, braces, repetitive paper laminations, neutral low-tier
   geometry, and declared fallbacks. Permit cleared donor adaptations only for
   bounded noncritical backing-bay parts, book or furniture surface details,
   and neutral dressing. Require explicit part maps, rights, transformation
   lineage, source and output hashes, and independent semantic companions.
   Donors and procedural forms may never define collision, contact edges,
   sockets, hazard motion, frame identity, or goal direction.
2. Build every visible frame fragment, module body, book, paper layer, brace,
   backing surface, dressing prop, material detail, and fallback entirely as
   custom Lost Pages art, forbidding donor and procedural support even when it
   carries no gameplay or identity role.
3. Start from the cleared donor inventory, assign the best-looking meshes and
   images to frame, landing, hazard, module, and handoff roles, then reshape
   semantic collision, sockets, route dimensions, and visual identity around
   those source assets.
4. Generate the complete frame, JR, modules, hazards, joins, backing, dressing,
   materials, and fallbacks from one procedural parameter system, using no
   custom hero modeling or bounded donor adaptations.

## Question 407: Critical Silhouette Authoring Order

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: Question 405 selects an integrated silhouette candidate and
Question 406 leaves its source split open, but an integrated pass still needs
one dependency order. The first authored dependency should protect canonical
JR scale, Jump readability, contact edges, and the fixed aperture rather than
letting outer-frame polish or isolated source dimensions rescale gameplay.

Question: Which custom-critical dependency should set the walking bay's
proportions first?

1. Build one `JR-to-Contact Readability Spine`. Lock canonical JR's final-scale
   silhouette, semantic companion, collider relationship, poses, and visible
   Jump arc first. Around that fixed JR and the accepted canonical units,
   author the safe landing, folded-page ledge, stitched bridge, book-spine
   stair, tearing-gap banks, living-paper ramp, hazard telegraphs, recovery
   marks, and safe-handoff contact silhouettes in route order. Prove contact,
   clearance, next-landing visibility, frame-anchored phone composition, and
   fallback parity inside the fixed `3:2` aperture. Only then complete the
   outer hero-frame fragments beyond the clearance envelope, structural
   backing, presentation joins, bounded support, materials, microdetail, and
   dressing. Later work may refine surfaces but cannot rescale JR, move
   semantic contacts, or change route edges.
2. Finish the complete hero frame, fragment composition, opening leaves,
   materials, reactions, and outer silhouette first, then scale JR, route
   modules, contacts, hazards, and recovery to whatever playable space remains.
3. Finish all fifteen `Archive Core` module silhouettes at independent
   standalone asset scales first, then choose six, resize them, and assemble a
   route around their exported dimensions.
4. Establish the final palette, materials, lighting, paper texture, donor
   dressing, particles, and key-art mood first, then derive JR scale, contact
   edges, hazard reads, module proportions, and frame clearance from the
   finished visual composition.

## Question 408: Backing-Bay Construction

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the walking bay uses one backing-bay module as its internal
structural root, but that module is not the physical wall-contact plane or
support hull and must not become a hidden source of collision, socket, or
placement authority. Its construction method should provide repeatable depth
and dressing support without changing semantic assembly across assets or
device tiers.

Question: How should the walking bay's backing-bay support geometry be built?

1. Use one build-time `Deterministic Archive Backplane`. Let the pinned
   semantic backing-bay definition own the root edge, transform sockets,
   dressing sockets, bounds, clearances, and structural role. A versioned,
   repo-owned declarative visual recipe consumes the accepted `W`, `H`, `J`,
   axes, and socket poses to generate shallow rear panels, perimeter rails,
   cross braces, paper laminations, and neutral support forms around those
   fixed semantics. It emits immutable inputs, tool version, output hashes,
   tier variants, and a simple accessible fallback. Cleared donor surface
   details may attach only through declared dressing sockets. No generated or
   donor mesh may define gameplay collision, contact tops, sockets, route
   topology, or physical wall fit. The separate O2 wall-contact backing plane
   and support hull remain authoritative. All tiers retain the same structural
   root and socket poses while reducing only neutral presentation.
2. Model one custom monolithic shadow-box mesh that bakes the backing,
   traversal modules, hazards, opening, and wall mounts together, using its
   exported pivot and mesh hierarchy as the route root and contact authority.
3. Use the internal node hierarchy of a passing staged frame or room donor as
   the backing-bay graph, deriving root, child sockets, brace positions,
   dressing mounts, and route depth from named or nearby mesh parts.
4. Generate a different backing arrangement at runtime from detected wall
   dimensions, device class, frame rate, and visible camera crop, allowing
   panel count, rail positions, braces, sockets, dressing mounts, and route
   depth to change after placement.

## Question 409: Walking-Bay Donor Intake Timing

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the staged MM_GDoC inventory currently contains one GLB and ten
textures, while Batch 078 already defines fail-closed provenance, role,
adaptation, part-map, and region-derivative contracts. The missing decision is
when those uncertain candidates may influence the walking bay without blocking
custom-critical work or forcing fixed semantic proportions to follow donor
dimensions.

Question: When should staged donor candidates enter the walking-bay workflow?

1. Use an `Early Gate, Late Bind` sequence. Run read-only provenance, rights,
   integrity, mesh or image, budget, and technical suitability gates on staged
   candidates as soon as an exact candidate is identified, but grant no route
   role or adaptation dependency yet. First lock the `JR-to-Contact Readability
   Spine`, semantic module definitions, and `Deterministic Archive Backplane`
   slots. Then issue one need-first role request for an exact noncritical
   backing part, surface detail, or neutral dressing region. Only a passing
   source may enter a versioned part- or region-level adaptation below those
   fixed semantics. Rejection or unavailability uses the declared custom or
   procedural fallback without moving contacts, sockets, route edges, frame
   identity, or milestone timing. The staged GLB and textures never sit on the
   candidate's critical path merely because they are present.
2. Bind the staged GLB and strongest frame textures before JR, route contacts,
   backing semantics, or aperture composition are fixed, then size and arrange
   the custom-critical work around their native dimensions and hierarchy.
3. Ignore provenance and technical review until the bay has final materials
   and lighting, then import any visually compatible staged assets directly as
   last-minute dressing without pinned roles, adaptations, fallbacks, or
   revalidation.
4. Permanently exclude every staged or future donor from the walking bay,
   requiring custom or procedural replacements even for neutral backing,
   noncritical surface detail, and dressing after a donor passes all gates.

## Question 410: Silhouette-to-Surface Handoff

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the general immutable handoff protocol is already provisional,
but the walking bay still needs one pass-specific exit artifact. Materials,
microdetail, motion, effects, and key art should not begin against silhouettes,
contacts, sources, or viewing fixtures that can still change without explicit
invalidation.

Question: What should authorize surface-development work on the walking-bay
candidate?

1. Require one hash-bound `Silhouette Readiness Packet`. Pin the candidate,
   prerequisite, semantic recipe, module, assembly, JR-to-contact spine,
   independent collision, hazard, recovery, handoff, aperture, frame-clearance,
   backplane recipe and output, source-role, donor-gate, adaptation, tier,
   fallback, and placeholder-classification identities. Bind deterministic
   replay plus browser and human-view, phone, physical-placement,
   accessibility, fallback, camera/crop, and performance evidence to those
   exact hashes. Record unresolved risks, limitations, producer result,
   consumer acceptance or rejection, and O5 applicability. O1 may open bounded
   surface, material, motion, effect, and key-art candidate nodes only after
   every required criterion passes. Any pinned source, semantic, silhouette,
   contact, camera, or evidence change makes affected downstream work stale.
   This handoff advances candidate development only; it cannot retire
   placeholders or grant production promotion.
2. Deliver a shared folder containing the latest GLBs, textures, screenshots,
   notes, and filenames, then let surface artists infer which revisions,
   contacts, sources, cameras, and tests remain authoritative.
3. Approve the silhouette pass from one attractive desktop video and several
   still images without binding deterministic replay, phone, physical,
   accessibility, fallback, performance, or exact-version evidence.
4. Start materials, microdetail, animation, effects, and key art in parallel
   with unresolved JR scale, route contacts, backing, source roles, and proof,
   then manually reconcile or repaint downstream work after those inputs move.
