Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the launcher currently embeds comic placeholder images and the

recognizable visual language across the magazine and playable AR worlds.

Question: What art language should the replacement imagery and scenes share?

1. Use a handcrafted impossible-museum mixed-media language: layered painted

## Question 323: Placeholder Classification

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the repo explicitly labels embedded comic images as
placeholders but also builds many scene objects from generic shape descriptors;
simple procedural art can be intentional, so production needs a reliable rule
that does not silently approve unfinished visuals.

Question: What should distinguish a shippable simple asset from a placeholder?

1. Require every visual binding to declare a typed manifest status such as
   `debug-placeholder`, `candidate`, `approved-authored`,
   `approved-procedural`, or `approved-staged-dressing`, plus its semantic role,
   exact source and rights record, revision and hash, supported tiers, and
   applicable proof. Permit deliberately authored simple or procedural art only
   after the same readability, spatial, accessibility, performance, and
   human-view gates; block production on missing, unknown, stale, or
   placeholder-critical bindings.
2. Treat every procedural shape as a placeholder and require an imported
   modeled asset for every platform, cue, background mark, and decorative prop.
3. Treat any image, model, or generated shape that loads successfully as
   production-ready without an explicit status, semantic binding, source
   record, or proof.
4. Let each reviewer decide by appearance whether an asset feels finished,
   without a manifest, stable classification, rejection reason, or automated
   production gate.

## Question 325: Placeholder Retirement

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the current comic placeholders are embedded in application
source and named in a public asset folder, so replacing what appears on screen
does not by itself prevent old bytes, caches, or fallback paths from silently
shipping or returning.

Question: How should replaced placeholder assets be retired?

1. Cut over atomically to one accepted content-addressed production catalog and
   verify that promoted routes contain no active or shipping placeholder
   bindings or bytes. Remove old embedded data and public runtime paths from
   the production bundle, while preserving their hashes, source and rights
   notes, replacement relation, and change evidence in a non-shipping record or
   explicit test fixture. Roll back only to a prior accepted production
   catalog; if approved art fails later, show the labeled accessible
   motif-and-text fallback rather than a placeholder.
2. Keep every embedded placeholder in the production bundle and silently show
   it whenever approved art is missing, corrupt, stale, or slow.
3. Delete the placeholder bytes together with every source, provenance,
   replacement, decision, and historical record, leaving no audit or bounded
   rollback evidence.
4. Ship old and new assets under unchanged identities and let browser cache,
   load order, or device timing decide which version appears.
