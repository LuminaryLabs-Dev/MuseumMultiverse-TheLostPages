# Picture-Frame Platformer Interview: Questions 386-390

## Question 386: MMGDoc Asset Adaptation Boundary

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the earlier hybrid-source choice allows selected museum assets
to support custom original hero work, but it does not yet define what a cleared
MMGDoc or staged source asset may become. A precise boundary is needed so local
availability never implies rights, visible mesh never becomes gameplay truth,
and adaptation remains reproducible across route packages and review evidence.

Question: How should cleared MMGDoc and staged museum assets be adapted into
the final hybrid kit?

1. Treat cleared assets as gated visual donors. After provenance, rights,
   integrity, and route-fit acceptance, allow a source asset to contribute a
   bounded support module, dressing element, material reference, or declared
   visual component. Normalize its coordinate frame, units, origin, pivot,
   bounds, materials, texture channels, level of detail, and performance tier;
   wrap it in an explicit visual binding over independent authored semantic
   volumes; preserve the source hash, attribution, transformation ledger,
   rights terms, route package, fallbacks, and review evidence. Reject rather
   than guess when any gate fails. Keep signature landmarks, route mechanisms,
   rewards, critical cues, JR, and story-defining silhouettes original to Lost
   Pages unless a separately authorized element-level source contract permits
   otherwise.
2. Treat every locally staged MMGDoc asset as production-ready because it
   already exists in the workspace, preserving its mesh, pivot, materials,
   scale, rights assumptions, and performance profile and deriving collision
   and interaction directly from it.
3. Freely kitbash, resculpt, recolor, and combine staged assets until their
   origins are visually unrecognizable, without retaining source hashes,
   transformation history, attribution, rights checks, or route-level
   fallback identity.
4. Exclude every museum or staged source asset even when it is cleared and
   technically suitable, requiring custom construction of all support,
   dressing, material references, and hero content.

## Question 387: Source Asset Route-Role Assignment

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the staged MMGDoc inventory includes a frame model and several
frame, painting, beam, and portal textures but no inspected source binds them to
a route. Assignment by filename, availability, or resemblance could distort
the authored eight-page curriculum, blur route identity, or make visual assets
silently drive semantic design.

Question: How should a cleared source asset be assigned to a Lost Pages route
and visual role?

1. Use one need-first route-role registry. Start with an approved route recipe
   and a typed visual-need brief that declares semantic display role,
   critical/support/dressing class, physical host family, canonical envelope,
   material and motif family, readability and accessibility duties, device
   budgets, required tiers, rights scope, and fallback. Let the asset gate
   compare cleared candidates against that brief and accept zero or one primary
   binding per candidate revision, with separately declared shared uses only
   when they preserve each route's identity and proof. Pin route, role, source
   hash, adapted revision, transformation record, rationale, rejection reasons,
   and evidence. If no candidate fits, use an approved procedural or custom
   replacement; never change mechanics, landmarks, or story to justify an
   available asset.
2. Inventory first: assign each cleared asset to whichever route it resembles,
   then reshape the route's mechanics, story, landmark, palette, and placement
   around the available model or texture.
3. Put every cleared asset in one global pool and let filenames, tags, runtime
   heuristics, or nearest visual similarity choose its route and role during
   loading.
4. Enforce a one-asset-per-route distribution quota so every cleared staged
   asset ships somewhere, even when it duplicates another route's identity,
   lacks a needed role, or would be better rejected.

## Question 388: Adapted Asset Authoring Source

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the provisional adaptation boundary requires a transformation
ledger, and existing architecture already invalidates transitive dependents
when an input changes. It does not yet define which editable source must
reproduce a normalized, optimized, materially restyled model or texture, so an
exported GLB or image could otherwise become an unexplained one-off.

Question: What should be the source of truth for each adapted MMGDoc or staged
museum asset?

1. Keep the accepted original bytes immutable and create one repo-owned,
   versioned adaptation package. Bind it to the source id, hash, rights record,
   and route-role assignment; include the editable DCC project or declarative
   transform recipe, required tool and plugin versions, units, axes, pivot,
   bounds, mesh operations, material and texture mapping, LOD and compression
   rules, export settings, accessibility and fallback bindings, attribution,
   and output hashes. Record authored sculpting or paint work as an explicit
   source revision and reproducible handoff rather than pretending every edit
   is deterministic. Every re-export creates a new candidate, reruns only
   affected gates through existing transitive invalidation, and leaves the
   accepted last-known-good package untouched until atomic promotion.
2. Keep only the final exported GLB, PNG, or texture atlas in the repo and
   discard the editable project, transform recipe, tool versions, export
   settings, intermediate source identity, and explanation of manual edits.
3. Treat an artist's external `latest` DCC file as authoritative, overwrite
   exports in place under stable filenames, and let visual review determine
   whether any unrecorded change matters.
4. Ship the accepted original asset plus a prompt or loose runtime script and
   reconstruct optimization, materials, pivots, LODs, and derivative textures
   on each device or build without pinned tools or output hashes.

## Question 389: Compound Asset Decomposition

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: a staged GLB may contain several nodes, meshes, primitives,
materials, transforms, cameras, lights, skins, or animations. Loading the whole
file as one object wastes budget and obscures roles, while automatically
treating its internal hierarchy as gameplay modules would violate the existing
semantic and renderer boundary.

Question: How should a compound accepted source asset be decomposed into
runtime visual modules?

1. Use one explicit non-destructive decomposition map inside the versioned
   adaptation package. Inventory the original scene graph and select only
   declared node, mesh, primitive, material, skin, and animation ranges for a
   typed visual need. Give each adapted output a stable visual-module id,
   route-role binding, native-to-canonical adapter, pivot, bounds, material and
   texture set, LOD and tier policy, byte budget, fallback, and source node
   paths or indices plus hashes. Permit one output to combine selected source
   parts or leave unused parts quarantined; deduplicate shared buffers through
   the package manifest. Bind each output to an independently authored semantic
   module and reject ambiguous hierarchy, hidden dependencies, unsupported
   features, or unexplained extras. Never let source node names, parentage,
   mesh bounds, or animation tracks define collision, sockets, interaction, or
   route topology.
2. Load the complete accepted file as one monolithic runtime object for every
   assigned role, preserving all unused meshes, cameras, lights, materials,
   animations, transforms, and dependencies and attaching semantic content
   around its visible bounds.
3. Automatically export every named node or submesh as a gameplay module,
   inferring platforms, hazards, sockets, interactions, and route connections
   from filenames, hierarchy, transforms, materials, and geometric proximity.
4. Destructively merge, split, delete, and reorder source parts until the asset
   looks right, then publish the new binaries without a source-part map,
   preserved original hierarchy, rejected-part record, or reproducible
   correspondence to the accepted source.

## Question 390: Image and Texture Donor Extraction

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: most of the staged MMGDoc inventory is PNG imagery for frames,
paintings, beams, portals, and glows. Whole-image reuse may preserve placeholder
composition, while unrecorded cropping, painting, or atlas assembly would lose
source lineage and could let pixels silently carry gameplay meaning.

Question: How should a cleared staged image contribute to route materials,
props, and visual effects?

1. Use one explicit region-level derivative map in the adaptation package.
   Preserve the original image and pin its source id, hash, rights, dimensions,
   color profile, alpha mode, and permitted transformations. Give every selected
   crop, mask, channel, vector trace, or painted derivative a stable id, exact
   source region or layer, authored diff, route-role binding, intended physical
   material scale or effect role, UV and channel contract, palette treatment,
   atlas placement and padding, mip and compression policy, supported tiers,
   fallback, attribution, and output hash. Treat manual restoration and
   paint-over as a new authored revision with explicit lineage. Re-author
   critical outlines, symbols, state masks, and text as independent approved
   cue carriers so source pixels, sampled colors, compression, or shader output
   never define collision, matching, interaction, progress, or eligibility.
2. Apply each accepted image whole as a billboard, decal, canvas, or emissive
   plane wherever it visually fits, preserving its original composition and
   resolution without region ids, physical material scale, route-role mapping,
   accessibility separation, or atlas and tier policy.
3. Automatically sample, segment, recolor, crop, and generate derivatives from
   staged images during builds or play, using detected shapes, brightness,
   palettes, text, and alpha to infer route cues, material identity, effects,
   interaction targets, and semantic state.
4. Manually cut, repaint, and combine accepted images into shared atlases, then
   keep only the flattened outputs without source-region maps, layer history,
   rights linkage, color and alpha metadata, transformation records, rejected
   regions, or reproducible derivative hashes.
