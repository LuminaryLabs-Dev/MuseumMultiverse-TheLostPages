## Question 421: First Backplane Layer

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: Batch 084 now provisionally gives backing and dressing one
accepted hero-frame revision, while the current generic scene has only a
`center-floor` course with no internal backing hierarchy. The first backplane
layer should establish repeatable archive depth without hiding the aperture,
route, JR, next cue, or frame-repair identity.

Question: Which layer should begin the deterministic archive backplane?

1. Begin with one `Clearance-First Structural Backplane Pass`. Generate a
   shallow neutral rear panel, perimeter rails, and the minimum cross-braces
   around the pinned backing semantic root, exact hero-frame attachment
   sockets, rear-depth envelope, aperture keepout, route/JR/cue visibility
   masks, wall-contact separation, and tabletop fallback. Keep every form
   non-traversable and noninteractive, use no donor dressing yet, and prove
   stable silhouette, clearance, shadow restraint, C1/C2/C3 and accessible
   static parity, phone Plan/Run crops, physical wall/tabletop views,
   performance, fallback, source, and deterministic regeneration before any
   shelves, books, papers, labels, props, atmosphere, or effects attach.
2. Bind the largest cleared shelf, cabinet, or room donor first, then derive
   the backplane root, panels, braces, attachment sockets, depth, aperture
   keepout, and frame clearance from that asset's exported shape.
3. Fill the rear volume immediately with complete shelves, books, papers,
   catalog cards, labels, conservation tools, donor props, and decorative
   clutter, then add only enough hidden structure to support the finished
   composition.
4. Establish final shadows, bloom, parallax, particles, and milestone lighting
   first, then place panels, rails, braces, shelves, and dressing wherever they
   best support the completed atmosphere.

## Question 422: Backplane Depth Zoning

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: Question 421 leaves the first backplane layer open, and the
current generic scene uses loose object `z` offsets rather than a typed rear
envelope. Backing and dressing need stable depth ownership so they cannot enter
gameplay clearance, block route/JR/cue visibility, or absorb the independent
wall-contact and support-hull authority.

Question: How should backing and dressing divide the rear-depth envelope?

1. Use one `Four-Zone Rear-Depth Contract`, ordered from the player-facing
   front toward the physical wall. Reserve `G0` as the protected
   gameplay/visibility void; permit only separately approved noninteractive
   leaves or effects there. Use `D1` as a shallow dressing-relief band with
   declared dressing-only sockets and bounded forward protrusion. Use `S2` for
   the neutral panel, rails, braces, and laminations. Keep `P3` as a
   physical-placement isolation band that ends before the independent O2
   wall-contact plane and support hull. Pin every near/far plane to the accepted
   frame root and `W/H/J` scale, allowed roles and socket types,
   aperture/route/JR/cue keepouts, occlusion and shadow ceilings, clearance,
   C1/C2/C3 reductions, accessible static form, tabletop fallback, and
   phone/physical proof. No generated or donor item may silently cross bands or
   gain traversal, interaction, placement, or collision authority.
2. Treat the entire rear envelope as one continuous decoration volume and let
   each artist or asset choose its own depth from visual fit, adjusting
   overlaps, shadows, route visibility, and wall clearance during final review.
3. Adopt the native depth layers and node hierarchy of the largest accepted
   shelf, cabinet, room, or frame donor, deriving dressing, structure, empty
   space, and wall separation from its exported bounds.
4. Compress, expand, reorder, or remove backing and dressing depth at runtime
   from detected wall size, camera crop, device tier, and frame time, allowing
   sockets, protrusion, shadows, and visible layers to change after placement.

## Question 423: First Dressing Family

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: Page 05's first walking bay already centers folded paper,
books, and bindings, while the current generic scene has no museum dressing at
all. The first dressing-only consumer should establish archive identity without
resembling a landing, bridge, hazard, checkpoint, interaction, or instruction.

Question: Which dressing family should attach first behind the walking bay?

1. Begin with one `Quiet Paper-and-Binding Study Set`. Bind a closed family of
   shallow flattened folio offcuts, closed book-spine clusters, pressed paper
   swatches, thread sample cards, and blank-edged catalog tabs only to declared
   `D1` dressing sockets outside aperture, route, JR, next-cue, repair, and
   gameplay-mark masks. Keep every item noninteractive, non-traversable,
   low-contrast, non-emissive, motionless, and free of readable instruction
   text. Pin item ids, physical scale, count and repetition ceilings, bounds,
   protrusion, source/rights/adaptation hashes, tier reductions, neutral
   fallback, late-streaming behavior, phone/physical crops, occlusion, shadow,
   and performance proof before other dressing families open.
2. Begin with a `Conservator Workbench Set` of clamps, brushes, bottles, knives,
   magnifiers, lamps, weights, and repair tools, making the working process the
   dominant background identity around the route.
3. Begin with an `All-Eras Artifact Sampler` of miniature statues, pottery,
   jewelry, masks, paintings, frames, fossils, and curios selected from every
   available cleared donor, even when their scales and visual languages differ.
4. Begin with an `Awakened Archive Set` of floating pages, opening books,
   twitching labels, glowing tools, drifting relics, dust trails, and reactive
   props that animate continuously around JR and the route.

## Question 424: Dressing Composition Map

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: the walking-bay route rises from its lower-left safe landing to
an upper-right handoff, so even an appropriate paper-and-binding family could
accidentally trace the route, fill the aperture, hide a landing, or compete
with the frame fragments. Dressing needs a stable composition rule across
placement, Plan, Run, phone, wall, and tabletop views.

Question: Which composition rule should place the first dressing family?

1. Use one `Protected Negative-Space Pocket Map`. Project the union of the
   aperture margin, complete route, JR envelope, next-cue corridor, hazards,
   recovery and handoff, frame-identity fragments, repair network, and
   accessibility masks through every accepted Plan/Run, phone/physical, wall,
   tabletop, and static fixture. Derive only from the stable complement up to
   three named `D1` dressing pockets, pin their root-relative bounds and
   sockets, and assign one low-relief cluster per pocket without mirroring the
   route diagonal. Pin per-pocket item, silhouette, edge-frequency, contrast,
   protrusion, occlusion, shadow, and performance ceilings plus deterministic
   C1/C2/C3 removal order and an empty-pocket fallback. Late arrival or failure
   may fill or clear only its pocket and cannot shift layout, alter depth, or
   gain gameplay meaning.
2. Cover the available rear panel with one evenly spaced wallpaper grid of
   repeated books, paper samples, thread cards, and catalog tabs, keeping equal
   density and contrast behind every part of the aperture and route.
3. Arrange the dressing as a second lower-left-to-upper-right diagonal that
   echoes every landing, bridge, hazard, recovery point, and handoff so the
   background visually repeats the playable path.
4. Scatter, rotate, scale, and cull the dressing procedurally per session from
   a random seed, current camera crop, room lighting, and frame time, accepting
   whichever negative spaces and clusters appear at runtime.

## Question 425: Backing-to-Atmosphere Handoff

Status: provisionally self-answered; user override remains open

Provisional selection: 1

Why it matters: Questions 421-424 define the proposed structural pass, depth
zones, first dressing family, and negative-space composition. The current
generic visual dataset has no backing/dressing boundary, so atmosphere,
landmark motion, and effects need one exact accepted consumer state rather than
a polished image or mutable asset folder.

Question: What should close backing and dressing and unlock atmosphere,
landmark motion, and effects?

1. Require one hash-bound `Backplane and Dressing Acceptance Packet`. Pin the
   prerequisite `Hero-Frame Cohesion Packet`; exact semantic root, structural
   recipe/tool/output hashes, `G0/D1/S2/P3` planes, sockets, bounds, keepouts,
   clearance, wall-contact separation, structural and dressing inventories,
   `Quiet Paper-and-Binding Study Set` items and source/rights/adaptation
   hashes, `Protected Negative-Space Pocket Map`, per-pocket ceilings,
   C1/C2/C3 and accessible static forms, late-stream/empty-pocket behavior,
   deterministic regeneration, phone/physical wall/tabletop,
   browser/human-view, occlusion, shadow, performance, fallback, risk, and
   producer/consumer verdicts. Unlock only one bounded `Atmosphere and Landmark
   Feedback Pass` that declares consumed hashes, immutable critical masks, and
   lighting, shadow, contrast, motion, particle, audio-intent, haptic-intent,
   reduced-motion, silent, static, and device budgets. Relevant dependency
   changes stale affected evidence. The packet grants no semantic, collision,
   placement, gameplay, microdetail, key-art, placeholder-retirement,
   promotion, push, or deployment authority.
2. Put the latest backplane, dressing assets, source notes, tier exports, and
   screenshots in one shared folder, then let the atmosphere and effects work
   infer the accepted revision, protected regions, budgets, and fallbacks.
3. Approve one polished full-frame beauty render with the backing and dressing
   visible, then treat it as sufficient proof for lighting, motion, effects,
   accessibility, tiers, streaming, physical hosts, and performance.
4. Start atmosphere, landmark motion, particles, and milestone effects before
   the backplane, depth zones, dressing family, pockets, streaming, and tier
   evidence settle, then repaint or reconcile every downstream dependency
   during final polish.
